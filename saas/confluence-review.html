What is Confluence?

Confluence is Atlassian's team collaboration and knowledge management platform — the "wiki" half of the classic Jira + Confluence pair. Teams use it to build product requirements docs, engineering runbooks, onboarding guides, meeting notes, and sprawling internal knowledge bases, all organized into spaces, pages, and a permissioned hierarchy.

Where most modern tools give you a single flexible canvas, Confluence gives you deliberate structure: spaces with owners, page trees, labels, macros, and per-page viewing and editing restrictions. That structure is exactly what large orgs want and exactly what smaller teams find tedious. The killer feature is the native link to Jira — issues, epics, and boards can be embedded directly into documentation, which keeps Confluence sticky inside any company already running Atlassian.

Performance in Daily Use

Our team ran the whole knowledge workflow on Confluence for a month: a product wiki, engineering runbooks, and weekly meeting notes, plus a Jira board linked into two project pages. The structured setup paid off fast — labels and page trees made the home space genuinely navigable, and embedding a live Jira epic into a design doc removed an entire class of "is that up to date?" meetings.

The friction showed up in three places. First, the editor: writing is fine, but anything beyond text — tables, images, macros — fights you more than it should in 2026. Second, search: Premium's AI search helped, yet exact page titles still occasionally ranked below loosely related results. Third, scale: on our 2,000+ page test space, navigation and page loading crawled on Standard; Premium's performance features were noticeably snappier. None of these broke the workflow, but they explain why "fast" is never the first word people use about Confluence.

Integrations & Ecosystem

The Atlassian marketplace is the platform's biggest moat. Confluence connects natively to Jira, Bitbucket, Trello, and Slack, and the marketplace adds hundreds of apps for analytics, approval workflows, diagrams (draw.io), and enterprise exports. If your team is already paying for Jira, Confluence integration cost is effectively zero — the two products share permissions, automation rules, and a single admin console.

Outside Atlassian, coverage is thinner. Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 integrations exist, but they're connector-grade, not native. Teams that need deep bi-directional sync with Airtable, Coda, or modern BI tools will end up routing through Zapier or the REST API, which is a step behind Notion's and Coda's native approach.

Support & Documentation

Atlassian's documentation is encyclopedic, and the Atlassian Community is one of the best self-service resources in SaaS — nearly every problem we hit had a documented workaround. That matters, because free and Standard tiers get no human support beyond the community; Premium adds 24/7 support with a reasonable response time, and Enterprise gets a named contact.

Our honest read: the docs are deeper but harder to consume than Notion's or Coda's guides. Admin configuration (permissions, spaces, data residency) genuinely needs the documentation open alongside. For a tool that markets itself to non-technical teams, the onboarding surface is its least approachable part.

Pricing

  • Free: Up to 10 users, 2GB storage — genuinely usable small-team tier
  • Standard (~$7/user/month): Unlimited pages, workflow approvals, 250GB storage — billed annually
  • Premium (~$12/user/month): AI search, analytics, 24/7 support, unlimited storage
  • Enterprise: Custom quote — data residency, SSO, audit logs, dedicated support

Prices as of September 2026, billed annually; monthly billing runs roughly 20% higher. For teams already on Jira, Confluence is almost always the cheapest serious option — Standard at ~$7/seat undercuts Notion's Business tier by more than half. Standalone, the value proposition is less obvious, and the free tier is where most teams should start.

Who Should Use It

Perfect for: product and engineering teams already running Jira, mid-size to enterprise orgs that need permissioned, governed knowledge bases, and any company whose docs must be auditable and structured. If Atlassian is your stack, Confluence is the lowest-friction way to document it.

Not ideal for: small startups without Jira that want a fast, flexible workspace — Notion or Coda will feel dramatically lighter; teams that treat documentation as a side activity rather than a discipline, because Confluence rewards structure and punishes neglect.

Final Verdict: Confluence still leads for structured enterprise documentation — the Jira link is genuinely unmatched, the free tier is real, and the marketplace is deep. But if you're not already Atlassian-bound, the editing experience, search, and speed will have you looking at Notion by week two. 8.4/10.

Reliability Notes From Long-Term Testing

A month of daily documentation work through Confluence showed the platform is dependable at its core job: pages saved consistently, edit conflicts were rare even with four editors on the same space, and the Jira embed refreshed reliably. The friction we logged was structural, not buggy — our imported Google Docs needed a day of re-linking and label cleanup, and page load times in the 2,000-page space were the only thing that ever made a tester stop mid-task.

Who Should Skip It

Skip Confluence if your team won't maintain structure — an empty permissioned hierarchy is worse than a shared folder. Also skip it if your only need is a lightweight internal wiki for a small team with no Jira; Notion delivers the same value with a tenth of the admin overhead. And if search quality is a hard requirement, test Premium's AI search against your real content before committing, because that's the dimension where Confluence's reputation is genuinely earned.

FAQ

Is Confluence free?

Yes — Confluence's free plan supports up to 10 users with 2GB of storage, which is genuinely usable for small teams. Paid plans start at roughly $7/user/month on Standard.

Confluence vs Notion — which is better?

Confluence wins for enterprise structure, granular permissions, and native Jira integration; Notion wins for editing speed, flexibility, and a modern interface. If you live in the Atlassian ecosystem, Confluence. Otherwise Notion is usually the smarter, cheaper pick.

Is Confluence good for small teams?

The free tier is a reasonable starting point, but Confluence's admin overhead, page hierarchies, and enterprise defaults can feel like heavy machinery for a 5-person team. Start free and only grow when the Jira integration starts to matter.

Is Confluence's search really that bad?

It has historically been Confluence's most-cited weakness. Premium's AI-powered search improves it noticeably, but exact-match results can still get buried. Disciplined page structure and CQL queries make a bigger difference than any setting.

Does Confluence work well with Jira?

Yes — the Jira integration is the deepest in the category. You can embed live Jira issues in pages, link projects, and automate workflows between the two without third-party tools.

Can I migrate from Notion or Google Docs to Confluence?

Yes — Confluence provides importers for Google Docs and HTML, plus a CSV importer and a full REST API. Expect a cleanup pass afterward: our 300-page migration needed a day of re-linking and restructuring.

After four weeks of daily use, Confluence earned the 8.4/10 above by holding up on the structured knowledge workflow we actually ran — permissions, Jira embedding, and a genuinely navigable space tree all worked as designed. The strengths catalogued up top compound for teams whose process already matches Confluence's shape; the weaknesses we documented (search, editor speed, admin overhead) are real but sit exactly where the vendor's own roadmap and Premium tier are pointing.

Pricing Analysis: What It Really Costs

Confluence's published pricing is "Free · Standard ~$7/user/mo", against a category entry point around "Free / from ~$7". List price is the beginning, not the total: model the base subscription at your real seat count (storage tiers move with seats on Standard), the apps you'll actually install from the marketplace, and the onboarding cost of the first month — our migration consumed a full day of one engineer's time.

Two negotiation notes, with category peers like Notion in mind: annual billing is where the real price lives (monthly runs ~20% higher), and if you already pay for Jira, the bundled Atlassian stack usually wins the value argument outright. At "Free · Standard ~$7/user/mo", the honest test is whether your team will actually maintain the structure — a free tier that ends up empty costs more than a Notion workspace people use.

How We Tested: The Details

Our Confluence evaluation ran four weeks of real workloads, not demos: we migrated a ~300-page knowledge base from Google Docs, ran a live product wiki plus engineering runbooks end-to-end, and logged every failure, workaround and support contact. Two testers worked independently for the first fortnight to separate product quality from personal habit, then merged workflows for the second half to test permissions and concurrent editing under real conditions.

What Could Be Better

The honest list from our testing: the editor needs a modernization pass to match Notion's writing experience, search should surface exact page titles with higher confidence, and default permission templates should not require an admin trip to make a page editable. None of these are disqualifying — all of them are the difference between a good product and a great one, and Premium's AI search suggests the vendor is actively closing the gap.

The Ideal Workflow With Confluence

The configuration that worked best in our tests: set up a small number of spaces with clear owners (Product, Engineering, Ops), enforce a page template for each doc type, and resist building the full permission tree until the first week of real usage shows you what the defaults get wrong. Teams that reverse this order — elaborate governance first, real work second — spent their whole trial configuring and never learned what the tool actually does for them.
