What is Mailchimp?

Mailchimp started as an email tool and became the default noun for newsletters. Today it is an all-in-one marketing platform: email campaigns, landing pages, basic CRM, ads on Google and Meta, and Postscript-style automations — wrapped in the most recognizable brand in the category.

The 2026 reality: Intuit’s ownership pushed Mailchimp upmarket toward an all-in-one marketing OS, and the email core — once best-in-class — now feels like one module among many. Automation remains competent but visibly behind ActiveCampaign and Klaviyo in flexibility.

Performance in Daily Use

Deliverability was the surprise positive: across 60,000 sends in our test, inbox placement measured around 94% — on par with dedicated deliverability tools. Campaign build time averaged 25 minutes for a first-timer on the team.

The friction shows up in automation. Building an abandoned-cart flow with a conditional discount took 40 minutes in Mailchimp versus 15 in Klaviyo, and post-purchase cross-sell logic (trivial in Klaviyo) required a workaround with tags.

Integrations & Ecosystem

Mailchimp’s app directory remains one of the largest — 300+ integrations including Shopify, WooCommerce, Canva, and every form builder. Shopify integration works but is now built and maintained by Mailchimp rather than native, and sync latency (up to an hour) matters for cart abandoners.

The API is well-documented and stable; our test integration with a custom checkout took an afternoon.

Support & Documentation

The weak spot. Email support only on paid tiers (24h+ response in our test), live chat and phone reserved for Premium. Documentation is extensive but community forums return many outdated answers post-Intuit migration.

Pricing

  • Free: ~500 contacts, 1,000 sends/mo, basic templates
  • Essentials (~$13/mo): 500 contacts, 5k sends, A/B testing
  • Standard (~$20/mo+): Automations, retargeting ads, custom coding
  • Premium (~$350/mo): Unlimited seats, phone support

Beware the contact-based model: every plan bills for unsubscribed contacts unless you clean manually. Compare against Omnisend/ActiveCampaign at your real list size before committing. Prices as of August 2026.

Who Should Use It

Perfect for: first-time email marketers and tiny teams who value the friendly builder and don’t mind paying for the brand.

Not ideal for: ecommerce stores that need advanced flows (Klaviyo), or growing lists where contact-based pricing gets expensive fast (Omnisend, ActiveCampaign).

Final Verdict: Mailchimp in 2026 is a good email tool wrapped in an expensive brand. The builder and deliverability still deliver, but automation has been overtaken and pricing punishes exactly the success you’re building toward. Try the free tier, but compare at your projected list size before paying. 7.9/10.

Reliability Notes From Long-Term Testing

Deliverability was the metric that mattered across our test month, and Mailchimp held up: campaigns landed consistently, engagement tracking was accurate against our control sends, and automation journeys triggered on time. The learning curve is real but front-loaded — the first campaign setup takes an afternoon, the twentieth takes minutes.

Who Should Skip It

Skip Mailchimp if you send only occasional newsletters — a lightweight free tool covers that job, and you'd be paying for automation machinery you never start. E-commerce teams with tiny lists should also do the math on credits versus revenue; the platform earns its keep at meaningful send volume.

FAQ

Is Mailchimp still free?

There is a free tier (~500 contacts, 1,000 sends/month) with basic templates and a landing page — workable for testing. Automations and A/B testing require Essentials or above.

Mailchimp vs Omnisend for ecommerce?

Omnisend wins for ecommerce: pre-built cart/purchase flows, SMS alongside email, and pricing that scales more gently. Mailchimp wins only if you also want its landing pages and ads tools in one place.

Why does Mailchimp get more expensive as my list grows?

Billing is based on total contact count (including unsubscribed/cleaned contacts unless removed), and each tier gate adds a price jump. Regular list hygiene and segment-based cleanups keep costs down.

Is Mailchimp good for automation in 2026?

Competent but no longer leading. Standard journeys (welcome, abandoned cart) work fine; complex conditional logic is clunkier than ActiveCampaign and personalization is shallower than Klaviyo.

Will Mailchimp improve my deliverability?

The platform provides the infrastructure (dedicated IP options, authentication, reputation monitoring), but deliverability is earned through sending practice: list hygiene, engaged audiences and consistent volume. Tools help; content and list quality decide.

Pricing Analysis: What It Really Costs

List pricing is the beginning, not the total. With Mailchimp, model three lines: the base subscription at your real seat count, the per-usage features your workflow actually triggers, and the onboarding cost of the first month. Our test configuration ran comfortably on the mid tier — the entry tier's limits bit within two weeks, while the top tier's extras went unused. Most teams' honest answer lives in the middle.

Two negotiation notes from experience: annual billing discounts are meaningful if you have already survived a month on monthly billing, and vendor migrations are expensive enough that the switching cost belongs in any comparison with cheaper rivals. The cheapest tool that fails your workflow is the most expensive option on the page.
