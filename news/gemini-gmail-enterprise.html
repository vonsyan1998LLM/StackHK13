Gemini Now Drafts, Summarizes, and Acts Inside Enterprise Gmail

Gmail has had AI assistants before, but they mostly composed emails on command. Gemini's new agentic layer doesn't just write — it acts, which is precisely why security teams should care.

StackHK News Desk·Updated Sep 4, 2026

Google is turning on deep Gemini integration across enterprise Gmail, adding one-click thread summaries with citations, tone-aware drafting, and agentic actions that can RSVP, schedule, and draft replies. The rollout begins today for Workspace Enterprise Plus customers and reaches all Gemini Enterprise plans over the next two weeks.

What's Happening

Google is switching on deep Gemini integration across enterprise Gmail, starting with Workspace Enterprise Plus today and expanding to all Gemini Enterprise plans within two weeks. The headline features are one-click thread summaries with inline citations, tone-and-length-aware drafting that borrows style from past emails, and a new 'agentic actions' layer that can RSVP to invitations, schedule meetings, and draft replies — all grounded in the specific conversation. Crucially, actions run under admin controls: IT can enable or disable autonomous behaviors per user group, and every action is logged to the same audit trail as normal mail. Google positions the update as Gmail's first true work agent, not another autocomplete. Rollout controls include a kill switch that suspends all agentic behavior workspace-wide in one click.

Why It Matters

Email remains the most-used application in the enterprise, which is why both Google and Microsoft have made it the frontline of the AI assistant war. Microsoft 365 Copilot has spent two years embedding itself in Outlook, and Gemini's Gmail integration gives Google a credible counter that is native to its own platform instead of bolted on. The step from drafting to acting is the significant one: summarizing and writing are safe, assistive features, while RSVPing and sending on your behalf introduce real autonomy and real risk. Google's bet is that enterprises will accept that risk when the model can point to exactly which email it acted on and why.

Key Numbers

Google says Gmail now serves more than three billion users across consumer and business accounts, with Workspace used by more than ten million paying organizations. The Gemini Enterprise add-on keeps its $30 per user per month price, and Google says there is no separate charge for the new agentic capabilities at launch. The rollout is staged over 14 days to allow admins to preview policies before general enablement, and support begins in English with 20+ languages promised by the end of 2026. Early internal tests, Google claims, cut average time to summarize a 40-message thread from about 25 minutes to under three. Google also released a reference benchmark showing the agent acting on 88% of test email requests correctly, with the remaining cases flagged for human review rather than silently failed.

Market Impact

The inbox is where the AI productivity war gets decided, because it is where workers actually live, and Google is now competing on agentic execution rather than drafting quality. For Microsoft, this raises the stakes on Copilot's Outlook roadmap; for the broader email-security industry, it creates a new surface: AI actions must be monitored, scoped, and rolled back just like any other privileged operation. IT departments will be deciding whether the autonomy is worth the governance burden, which is why Google is making DLP and retention policies the gating layer for agent actions. If enterprise customers adopt 'execute' mode at scale, the definition of email management software changes permanently.

What To Watch

Watch whether organizations actually enable autonomous sending or keep the human-review default, because that single toggle determines how disruptive this release is. Also track the compliance response: regulated industries will want to know how citation-backed summaries survive legal hold and e-discovery, and whether agent actions are fully auditable. The language rollout matters too, since non-English workplaces are the fastest-growing Workspace segment. Finally, watch Microsoft's answer — Copilot in Outlook is the direct competitor — and whether Google bundles these capabilities into a Workspace price increase in 2027. The most telling signal will be whether summaries with citations reduce 'reply-all storms' enough that users notice the difference in their day.

The Key Facts

  • Deep Gemini integration ships today to Workspace Enterprise Plus, expanding to all Gemini Enterprise plans within two weeks.
  • Thread summaries include inline citations that jump to the exact email the model used; drafting adapts to tone and length.
  • Agentic actions can RSVP, schedule, and draft replies under admin controls, with a workspace-wide kill switch.
  • Every agent action is logged to the mail audit trail and respects existing DLP and retention policies.
  • No price change: the Gemini Enterprise add-on stays at $30 per user per month; English at launch, 20+ languages by end of 2026.
  • Google cites a 14-day staged rollout and claims a 40-message thread can be summarized in under three minutes.
