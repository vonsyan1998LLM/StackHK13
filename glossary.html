AI Glossary, in plain English

Every term you'll meet in reviews and news posts — explained without the jargon.

A

Agent

An AI system that can plan and take multi-step actions toward a goal — browsing, calling tools, writing files — rather than just answering once.

ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition)

Software that converts spoken audio into text. The engine behind dictation tools and meeting transcribers.

C

AGI (Artificial General Intelligence)

A hypothetical AI system that matches or exceeds human cognitive abilities across virtually all domains. Current LLMs are narrow AI — powerful within their training domain but not genuinely general. AGI remains theoretical and is the long-term goal of several research labs.

AI Watermarking

A technique for embedding detectable signals in AI-generated content (text, images, audio) to identify its origin. Anthropic began embedding watermarks in Claude outputs in August 2026. Detection requires access to the generating model or a detection tool.

Benchmark

A standardized test (MMLU, Terminal Bench, HumanEval) measuring model capability. Useful for direction, dangerous as gospel: benchmarks leak into training data and rarely match your actual workload.

Context Window

How much text a model can consider at once — conversation history, documents, instructions. Bigger windows handle longer projects.

Chain-of-Thought

Prompting a model to reason step by step before answering, which improves accuracy on complex problems.

D

Diffusion Model

The technique behind most AI image generators: starting from noise and gradually refining it into an image that matches your prompt.

F

Distillation

Training a smaller “student” model to replicate a larger “teacher” model’s behavior. Enables deploying capable AI at lower cost and latency — the technique behind many “mini” or “flash” model variants.

Embedding

A numerical vector representation of text, images or other data that captures semantic meaning. Words or documents with similar meanings have similar embeddings — the foundation of semantic search and RAG systems.

Fine-tuning

Additional training on your own examples to specialize a model's tone, format, or domain knowledge.

H

Function Calling

The mechanism that lets an LLM trigger external tools — search a database, call an API, write a file. It is the technical foundation every AI agent is built on.

GPT

Generative Pre-trained Transformer — the architecture behind OpenAI’s models and the reason "GPT" became shorthand for AI chatbots. Today it also refers to custom chatbot instances built on those models.

Guardrails

Safety layers around a model — input filters, output checks and topic policies that block harmful or off-brand responses. Businesses deploy their own guardrails on top of vendor-provided ones.

Hallucination

When an AI states something false with confidence. Why citations, sources, and human review still matter.

L

Inference

The act of running a trained model to produce output. Inference speed and cost are what you actually pay for when calling an AI API, measured in tokens per second and dollars per million tokens.

LLM (Large Language Model)

The core technology behind chatbots like ChatGPT and Claude — trained on vast text to predict and generate language.

M

LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)

A parameter-efficient fine-tuning technique that trains small “adapter” layers instead of the full model. Enables customizing large models on consumer hardware at a fraction of full fine-tuning cost.

Multimodal

Models that work across formats — reading images, hearing audio, watching video — not just text in, text out.

P

MoE (Mixture of Experts)

An efficiency architecture where only specialized "expert" subsets activate per request — how a model can hold 284B parameters while activating just 13B, cutting cost without cutting capability.

Open Weights

Models whose learned parameters are publicly downloadable, letting anyone run, fine-tune or self-host them. Not the same as open-source (training code and data usually stay private). Qwen and DeepSeek are prominent examples.

Prompt Engineering

Designing instructions that reliably get the output you want. Better prompts beat better models more often than people expect.

R

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

Grounding a model's answers in your own documents by retrieving relevant passages first — the cure for hallucinated facts.

T

Synthetic Data

Artificially generated training data created by AI models rather than collected from real sources. Used to augment limited datasets, but carries risks of reinforcing model biases or degrading quality through recursive training.

System Prompt

Instructions given to an AI model that define its behavior, personality, constraints and output format before the user’s input is processed. System prompts shape every response but are invisible in standard chat interfaces.

TTS (Text-To-Speech)

Generating natural-sounding speech from text. Modern TTS handles emotion, pacing, and voice cloning.

Token

The unit of text models read and bill by — roughly three-quarters of a word in English.

Transformer

The neural network architecture introduced in the 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need." Its attention mechanism lets models weigh every word against every other word — the foundation of all modern LLMs.

Training Data

The text, images and code a model learns from. Its quality, freshness and licensing determine what a model knows — and it is the center of most AI copyright debates.

Temperature

A parameter controlling the randomness of AI model outputs. Lower values (0–0.3) produce focused, deterministic responses; higher values (0.7–1.0) increase creativity and variety but reduce consistency.

Vector Database

A database optimized for storing and searching embeddings by similarity rather than exact match. Powers semantic search, RAG systems and recommendation engines. Examples include Pinecone, Weaviate and pgvector.

VLM (Vision Language Model)

A multimodal AI model that processes both images and text — enabling tasks like image description, visual question answering, chart analysis and UI understanding. GPT-5.6, Claude and Gemini all include vision capabilities.

Zero-shot / Few-shot

Prompting styles: zero-shot asks the model to perform with no examples; few-shot includes a handful of demonstrations. A few good examples still beats clever instructions for consistent formats.
